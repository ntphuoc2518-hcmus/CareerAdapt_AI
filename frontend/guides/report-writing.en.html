Writing an evidence-based analysis report

An internal CareerAdapt AI guide that follows the criteria of task DA03. About 45 minutes of reading and practice. The examples use made-up figures.

1. Open with the conclusion

Busy readers need the most important point first. State the conclusion in the first sentence, with the figure that proves it.

April revenue fell 12% from March, from VND 500 million to VND 440 million.

2. Support it with reconciled figures

Every claim needs a figure. Before writing, reconcile the sum of the parts with the overall total. Always state the unit and the point of comparison.

3. State the direction of the trend correctly

Check the sign of the change and the base month of the division. Use the same formula for every period you compare.

4. Recommend actions tied to the data

A good recommendation says who does what, and because of which figure. Where possible, name the metric and the deadline for measuring the result.

The sales team should review the 20 most recent cancelled orders this week and track the cancellation rate over the next 4 weeks.

5. State the limits of the data

Say clearly what the data cannot yet support. A small sample or a short period does not allow generalisation. Data shows what happened, and often not why.

The analysis rests on only two months of data, so it cannot yet conclude why revenue fell.

6. Check before sending

  • Does the first sentence state the conclusion?
  • Has every figure been checked against its source?
  • Is there a specific recommendation?
  • Are the limits stated, and are unproven causal claims avoided?
  • Is the report within the required length?
